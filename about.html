We move resources to where they change a life.

RobinHood is a national 501(c)(3) nonprofit headquartered in Denver, Colorado, working in 12 states with 146 community partners and a staff of 214.

Mission & vision

Our mission

To end the cycle of poverty in American communities by funding, building and running programs that deliver measurable, lasting stability for families — and by holding ourselves publicly accountable for the results.

Our vision

A country where a hard month never becomes a lost decade. Where a parent who loses a shift does not lose an apartment. Where the zip code a child is born into stops predicting the life they get.

How we are different

We do three things most organizations pick only one of: we operate direct-service programs, we fund local partners who know their blocks better than we ever will, and we publish the evidence — including the pilots that failed and the ones we shut down.

214 staff across 5 regional offices

What guides us

Five values we are willing to be measured against

These are not wall decorations. Each one maps to a policy, a budget line or a published metric.

Evidence over instinct

No program scales past a pilot without an external evaluation. We budget 4% of program spending for measurement, every year.

Local first

Decisions are made in the community, not in Denver. Regional directors control 70% of their own program budget.

Every dollar accounted for

92 cents of each dollar reaches programs. Our audit, Form 990 and executive compensation are published in full.

Dignity, not charity

Client-choice pantries, private intake rooms, no sermons and no hoops. People know what their family needs.

Stay until it holds

We track families for 24 months after program exit. A win that unravels in month seven was never a win.

Say the hard thing

We publish what did not work. Three programs have been closed since 2016 because the data did not justify the spend.

Our story

From one pantry on Colfax to 12 states

In 2009, caseworkers Dana Whitfield, Marcus Reyes and Elena Chu were watching the same families come through emergency services three and four times a year. The system was designed to respond to crises, not to end them.

They rented a storefront, stocked it with produce from a wholesaler willing to take a late payment, and made one rule: anyone who walked in chose their own groceries. Seventeen years later, that rule still governs 84 pantries.

  • 2009

    The first Fresh Start pantry opens

    1,900 square feet on East Colfax Avenue, staffed entirely by volunteers, serving 60 households a week.

  • 2013

    Housing work begins

    After a study found that 41% of pantry clients were behind on rent, RobinHood launches deposit assistance and eviction defense.

  • 2017

    Career Launch and the first outcome audit

    Workforce training starts in Denver and Phoenix, and we publish our first externally audited outcome tables — including a pilot we closed.

  • 2021

    National expansion

    Regional offices open in Atlanta, Cleveland and Phoenix. The partner network passes 100 organizations.

  • 2026

    Today

    Six program lines, 12 states, 214 staff, and 4.2 million meals distributed since the doors first opened.
